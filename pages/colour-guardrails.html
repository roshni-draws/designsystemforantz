Approved pairs and their contrast

Each text or UI colour on the background it is approved for. AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text; AA large (24px, or 19px bold) and UI parts such as icons, borders, rails and focus rings need 3:1. Gradients count at their worst stop. The sample shows the current theme.

Limits

  • One feature fill per screen: green, charcoal, blue, teal, mint, glow or aurora. Everything else is a white card.
  • Accent budget: strong colour (accent, solid status, feature fill) covers about a tenth of a screen at most. One primary button per view or card.
  • One strong colour per card. Tints and icon tiles carry the category; the card stays white.
  • Three status colours at most visible in one card, and one danger element unless the card lists errors.
  • Six hues at most in one chart or legend. Beyond that, group the rest as "Other" in neutral.

Every status carries a word; alerts add an icon and a title; the priority picker uses one, two or three exclamation marks; the mortality card uses a tag and a dated line as well as the red rail.

Dark mode

  • Swap by role, never by value: semantic tokens change between themes, primitives don't. Never hard-code a light hex.
  • Surfaces get lighter as they rise: board, canvas, card, raised. Shadows do less, so keep the step between surfaces.
  • The accent turns lime, and text on it turns dark green. Don't put white on the dark accent.
  • Text on tints uses the -ink tokens, which turn pastel in dark. Base hues for green, violet and navy lighten so icons and series keep 3:1.
  • Feature fills (deep green, charcoal, gradients) stay the same in both themes, and so does the white or lime text on them.
  • Check both columns of the table above before using a new pair.

  • Series take the base hues in this order: green, teal, sky, amber, violet, coral, navy. All of them reach 3:1 on a card in both themes.
  • Use -soft for areas and bands behind a series, and -ink for labels on those areas.
  • Keep success, warning and danger for thresholds, such as "over capacity", never for categories.
  • Label series directly or with a legend that names them. From the fifth series on, add markers or line styles as well.
  • Gridlines use --color-border-subtle; axis text uses --color-text-muted.

Where brand green goes

Allowed
  • Primary buttons, selected and active states, the logo tile
  • Progress, success status, the one deep green feature card
  • Lime figures and highlights on deep green
Not allowed
  • Body text, page backgrounds beyond the mint board, big decorative areas
  • A chart's "bad" series, or categories where a hue fits
  • Recolouring partner or third-party logos

Colour guardrails

Do
  • Check a new pair in the table, in both themes, before using it.
  • Pair every status colour with a word or an icon.
  • Spend strong colour on the one thing that matters on a screen.
Don't
  • Use a hue to mean a status.
  • Put lime, a base hue or coral-500 text on white.
  • Use more than one feature fill on a screen.

Colour guardrails

The contrast table on the Overview tab is generated. After changing a colour token, run:

python3 tools/colors.py build      # write colors.json
python3 tools/colors.py check      # compare it with tokens.css
python3 tools/colors.py contrast   # print every pair
python3 tools/colors.py html       # refresh the table on this page
